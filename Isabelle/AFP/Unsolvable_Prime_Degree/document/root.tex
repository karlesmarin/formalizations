\title{Unsolvable Polynomials of Every Prime Degree}
\author{Carles Mar\'in}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We prove the classical criterion that an irreducible polynomial over $\mathbb{Q}$ of prime degree $p\ge 5$ with
exactly two non-real roots has a non-solvable Galois group and is therefore not solvable by radicals. Complex
conjugation transposes the two non-real roots and irreducibility makes the action on the roots transitive. The proof
builds on the entry on the Abel--Ruffini theorem~\cite{Unsolvable_Quintic-AFP}, whose example quintic is an instance of the criterion with $p=5$. As an
application, for every prime $p\ge 5$, with $k=(p-3)/2$, the polynomial
$x\,(x^2+2(k+1)^3)\prod_{i=1}^{k}(x^2-4i^2)-2$ of degree $p$ is irreducible by Eisenstein's criterion at $2$, has
exactly two non-real roots, and is not solvable by radicals.
\end{abstract}
